Privacy policy

In short

  • Rurana has no server of its own. The app exchanges your data only with the Vikunja servers you add, or with the public demo if you try it.
  • The app contains no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no crash reporting.
  • I, the developer, never receive your tasks, your account or anything about how you use the app.
  • I process personal data myself only when you write to me, take part in a test or visit this website, and when the app stores give me statistics.

The app

Your Vikunja server

Everything you see and enter in Rurana — tasks, projects, comments, attachments, your profile and your settings — comes from and goes to the Vikunja server you signed in to. Whoever runs that server (you, your organisation or a hosting provider) is responsible for the data on it, and their privacy policy applies. If the server names one, the app shows it on the Account tab under Server.

Like any app that connects to a server, Rurana sends your device's IP address with every request. It also tells the server its own name, version and build, and the name and version of your device's operating system; the server can show this in your list of sessions.

Rurana uses encrypted connections (HTTPS). For an address that starts with http://, it reads only the server's public information until you allow the unencrypted connection, for example on your home network; after that, anyone on that network can read what is sent, the sign-in tokens included.

Links in tasks and comments open only when you tap them. Images from other websites in descriptions and comments are never loaded.

Signing in

You sign in on your server's own sign-in page, which the app opens in your device's browser. Rurana never sees your password or your two-factor code. The server gives the app tokens for your session, which it keeps in the device's secure storage (the Keychain on iOS, storage protected by the Android Keystore on Android). Creating an account, resetting or changing your password, changing your email address and deleting your account also happen on your server's web pages.

The public demo

If you tap “Try the public demo”, Rurana connects to try.vikunja.io, a demo server run by the Vikunja project, not by me. The privacy policy of Vikunja applies there. The demo is public and is reset from time to time, so do not enter personal data there.

What stays on your device

So that Rurana works offline and quickly, it keeps the following in its own storage on your device:

  • for each account, a copy of your projects, tasks and notifications from the server, changes and uploads not sent yet, comment drafts, and attachments you opened (in a cache that is emptied after three days);
  • the sign-in tokens and, if you set them up, your servers' access headers and client certificates, in the secure storage (Keychain or Keystore);
  • the list of your accounts with server address, username and name, the connection settings, your settings on this device, and a description you were editing until you save or discard it;
  • the content of the home-screen widgets, and the reminders handed to the system as notifications.

None of this leaves your device, apart from what the app sends to your server. When you sign out of an account or remove it, Rurana deletes that account's copy of your data, its comment drafts, its widgets' content, its notifications and its tokens from the device; changes not sent yet are lost, and the app warns you first. When signing out, Rurana also asks the server to end the session. Deleting the app deletes its data. On iOS the system can keep the tokens in the Keychain; Rurana deletes them the first time it starts after being installed again. If you delete the app without signing out, the server ends the session once it has gone unused for three days; you can also end it in Rurana on another device under Account settings › Sessions.

Backups: on Android, Rurana's data is excluded from backups and from transfers to a new device. On iOS, the copy of your tasks and the widgets' content are excluded from backups; your settings, the account list and drafts are part of the device's backup. The tokens never move to another device.

Permissions

Rurana asks only for permissions that one of its features needs:

Notifications
Reminders and due dates of your tasks, and activity from your server. The app creates them on the device from its copy of your data; no push service is involved.
Exact alarms (Android)
So that reminders appear on the minute. Without them, Android may show reminders a few minutes late.
Running after a restart (Android) and in the background
So that the app can plan your reminders again after a restart and sync with your server from time to time while it is closed.
Face ID, fingerprint or screen lock
Only for the app lock, if you turn it on. The system checks that it is you; Rurana only learns whether unlocking succeeded.
Camera
For photos you attach to a task or use as your avatar. On Android, the system's camera app takes the photo.
Photos and files
Rurana receives only the photos and files you choose in the system's picker. On iOS, the system asks for your permission the first time you save an attachment to your photos.
Local network (iOS)
So that the app can reach a server on your home or office network.

On iOS, Rurana removes the location from photos and videos you choose from your photos or take with the camera before it uploads them. On Android, the system's photo picker usually removes it. Files you choose as files or share to Rurana from other apps, and photos taken with the camera app on Android, are uploaded as they are and can contain the location. Rurana never reads your device's location.

Notifications and widgets can show task titles on the lock screen and the home screen. You choose what they show on the Account tab under App lock and privacy.

App stores and test versions

Google Play and the App Store process downloads, updates, ratings and diagnostics as their own responsibility, under the privacy policies of Google and Apple. Depending on your device's settings, they give me statistics and crash reports that do not tell me who you are. I use them to find and fix defects (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; my legitimate interest is an app that works). They stay in the stores' consoles for as long as the stores keep them.

If you take part in a test version through Google Play or TestFlight, I see the email address you were invited with, where the store shows it to me, and the feedback you send. I use them only to run the test (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and delete the list of testers when the test ends.

If you download the APK file from GitLab, GitLab's privacy statement applies to the download.

When you write to me

If you email me, I use your email address and your message to answer you (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, or Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR where your message concerns using the app). My email provider processes the messages on my behalf. I delete them once your request has been dealt with, unless the law requires me to keep them longer.

If you report a problem in Rurana's GitLab project, GitLab processes what you post under its own privacy statement, and I see it like other visitors. Issues are public unless you mark them as confidential.

This website

This website is hosted on GitLab Pages, a service of GitLab Inc. in the USA. When you open a page, GitLab records your IP address, the time and the page, and deletes these logs after seven days. This is needed to deliver the website and to protect it from attacks (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). GitLab is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework; see GitLab's privacy statement. The website sets no cookies, loads nothing from other servers and has no analytics and no tracking.
